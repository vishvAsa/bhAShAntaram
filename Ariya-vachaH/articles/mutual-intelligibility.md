As an example:

Old Persian:
yaϑā mām kāma, avaϑā-diš akunavam

Sanskrit:
yáthā máma (/me) kāmás, táthainān (/kāmò, 'múthainān) akṛṇavam (/akaravam)

Dramatic Prakrit:
ja(d)hā mama (/me/maha/majjha/majjhu/majjaṃ) kāmo ta(d)hā haṃ (ahaṃ/aha(g)äm/ahammi/amhi) ime kāhīa (/kāsī/kāhī)

Neither is mutually intelligible.

Prakrit has remodeled/simplified the verbal system enough that the cognate Sanskrit morphology isn't transparent.

Old Persian has an entirely different correlative (i.e. avaϑā) and uses the Iranic anaphoric enclitic "-di."

While the enclitic "-di" exists in Baltic (Old Prussian) as well and can therefore likely be reconstructed to PIE, it doesn't exist in Indo-Aryan. 

Unlike Sanskrit, Old Persian also uses the unmarked "-a" form for the nominative and compensates for he loss of an explicit nominative marker by marking the vocative with long -ā (e.g. "martiyā," O man!), akin to the Sanskrit pluta prolongation, which is typically employed when calling someone from a distance (cf. dūrād dhūte ca, 8.2.84)
