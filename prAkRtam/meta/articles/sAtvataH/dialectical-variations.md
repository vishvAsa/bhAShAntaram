There was dialectical variation among the early MIA Prakrits even at the time of Aśoka, so Aśoka used slightly different MIA variants in different regions.
However, these inscriptions ultimately reflect actual spoken 3rd century BC MIA dialects.

It is "Pāli" and "Prakrit" which are literary composites.

There are 31 ways to say "he/she/it shall be" (bhaviṣyánti) in "Prakrit":
hohinti, hohinte, hohire, hossanti, hossante, hoihinti, hoihinte, hoihire, hoehinti, hoehinte, hoehire, hoissanti , hoissante, hoessanti, hoessante, hojjahinti, hojjahinte, hojjahire, hojjāhinti, hojjāhinte, hojjāhire, hojja, hojjā, hoejjahinti, hoejjahinte, hoejjahire, hoejjāhinti, hoejjāhinte, hoejjāhire, hoejja, hoejjā.

There are 18 ways to say Bṛ́haspáti- in "Prakrit":
bhiassai-, bhuassai-, bhaʸassai-, bihassai-, buhassai-, bahassai-, bhiapphai-, bhuapphai-, bhaʸapphai-, bihapphai-, buhapphai-, bahapphai-, bhiappai-, bhuappai-, bhaʸappai-, bihappai-, buhappai-, bahappai-.

There are seven ways to say "it may be" in Pāḷi:
bhaveyya, bhave, huveyya, hupeyya, heyya, siyā, and assa

There are seven possible locative singular forms of "mātu-" (mother) in Pāḷi:
mātari, mātuyā, matyā, mātuyaṃ, matyaṃ, mātumhi, mātusmiṃ

This variation is the result of leveling multiple dialectical variants into one literary register.
