## Convergent evolution
I wade into this with some trepidation, given how many iterations we have been through and how much emotional valence views on this topic can hold. While such a view is widely held among our people, one has to sit back and consider what are fundamentally evolutionary problems using principles appropriate to that class of problems.  

For a loose analogy, the person uninformed in such principles might see the linsang as a civet rather than closer to the cats or may fail to see that hippos are closer to whales than to pigs. 

## Evolutionary tree
One needs a proper evolutionary framework to construct a distance metric to evaluate such questions. In the case of Neo-Indo-Aryan languages, one cannot see them in isolation. One has to first compare them to their immediate ancestral apabhramsha-s, then the Prakrits and then finally Sanskrit. 

The final step has to be done keeping in mind that Old Indo-Aryan already had a dialectual divide roughly corresponding to Grierson and more recently Zoller's schemes, and the multiple waves of invading Aryans. 

For the OIA situation, also see: [TW](https://manasataramgini.wordpress.com/2023/12/14/some-historico-linguistic-considerations-on-indo-aryan/)

## OIA NIA proximity
Thus, once one establishes the connections via the Prakrits to the OIA dialects that were ancestors of the different NIA languages, one can easily see that there is a shorter distance between them and OIA as opposed to the Dravidian tongues.
